## Define the useful result

Before adding AI to a workflow, describe what a good result looks like. “Help engineers investigate faster” needs a more precise definition: collect relevant evidence, summarize a change, or suggest the next question.

A narrow task is easier to evaluate and easier to fit into an existing practice.

## Keep the evidence visible

An AI-generated summary is easier to review when it points to the material behind its claims. Engineers need enough context to check a conclusion, understand uncertainty, and correct mistakes.

For an investigation workflow, that might mean preserving timestamps, linking to logs, and distinguishing observations from proposed explanations.

## Evaluate the whole workflow

A convincing example does not tell you how a tool behaves across everyday cases. Include ambiguous inputs, missing context, and cases where the appropriate response is to ask for more information.

Ask whether the workflow saves useful effort after review and correction are included. Keep consequential actions subject to the judgment of the people responsible for the system.

Useful AI starts with a clear problem and earns its place through outcomes people can inspect.
